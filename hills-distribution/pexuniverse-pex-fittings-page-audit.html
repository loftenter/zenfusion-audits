Q1: How much does a PEX fittings cost?
A quality PEX fittings typically ranges from $300 to $3,000 depending on type and capacity. High-efficiency systems with advanced metered valves typically run $1,200-$2,000. Installation adds $200-$600 if hiring a plumber, though many modern systems support DIY installation. Annual maintenance costs (salt or alternative) run $100-$300 depending on water hardness and household size.
Q2: What is the ROI of a PEX fittings?
A PEX fittings typically pays for itself within 2-4 years through reduced appliance replacement costs. Hard water causes scale buildup that shortens appliance lifespans by 30-50%. Softened water also reduces soap and detergent usage by up to 50%, saving $200-$600 per year. Combined with extended appliance lifespans, a $1,500 system can return its investment in under 3 years.
Q3: Do PEX fittingss affect septic systems?
Modern PEX fittingss are safe for septic systems when properly configured. The salt brine discharge is diluted enough that it doesn't harm the bacterial balance. High-efficiency systems produce less discharge than older models, further reducing any septic impact. If your septic system is undersized or failing, consult a septic professional before installation.
Q4: Can I install a PEX fittings myself or do I need a plumber?
Many modern PEX fittingss are designed for DIY installation with push-to-connect fittings or bypass valves — typically a 2-4 hour job. You'll need access to the main water line, a drain for regeneration discharge, and a nearby electrical outlet. For homes with older plumbing or complex configurations, a licensed plumber is recommended. Improper installation can void your warranty.
Q5: How does a PEX fittings affect drinking water taste?
Softened water has a slightly different taste due to the ion exchange process. Most people don't notice the change. The sodium added is minimal: roughly 7.5 mg per 8 oz glass for moderately hard water (10 GPG) — less sodium than a slice of bread. For those sensitive to taste, a bypass valve for the kitchen tap or a reverse osmosis system can provide untreated drinking water.
Q6: Can a PEX fittings be installed outside?
Yes, PEX fittingss can be installed outdoors in mild climates with proper protection from direct sunlight, freezing temperatures, and heavy rain. A weather-resistant enclosure is recommended. In areas where temperatures drop below freezing, indoor installation in a garage, basement, or utility closet is preferred.
