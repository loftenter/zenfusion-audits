Q1: How much does a pex tubing cost?
A quality pex tubing typically ranges from $300 to $3,000 depending on type and capacity. High-efficiency systems with advanced metered valves typically run $1,200-$2,000. Installation adds $200-$600 if hiring a plumber, though many modern systems support DIY installation. Annual maintenance costs (salt or alternative) run $100-$300 depending on water hardness and household size.
Q2: What is the ROI of a pex tubing?
A pex tubing typically pays for itself within 2-4 years through reduced appliance replacement costs. Hard water causes scale buildup that shortens appliance lifespans by 30-50%. Softened water also reduces soap and detergent usage by up to 50%, saving $200-$600 per year. Combined with extended appliance lifespans, a $1,500 system can return its investment in under 3 years.
Q3: Do pex tubings affect septic systems?
Modern pex tubings are safe for septic systems when properly configured. The salt brine discharge is diluted enough that it doesn't harm the bacterial balance. High-efficiency systems produce less discharge than older models, further reducing any septic impact. If your septic system is undersized or failing, consult a septic professional before installation.
Q4: Do pex tubings affect water pressure?
A properly sized pex tubing should not noticeably reduce water pressure. Systems with 1-inch valves maintain pressure even during peak usage. If you notice reduced pressure after installation, it may indicate the system needs regeneration, the resin bed needs cleaning, or the softener is undersized for your household's peak flow rate.
Q5: How does a pex tubing affect drinking water taste?
Softened water has a slightly different taste due to the ion exchange process. Most people don't notice the change. The sodium added is minimal: roughly 7.5 mg per 8 oz glass for moderately hard water (10 GPG) — less sodium than a slice of bread. For those sensitive to taste, a bypass valve for the kitchen tap or a reverse osmosis system can provide untreated drinking water.
